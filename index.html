Engineering + Founder Roadmap

A four-year operating system for becoming an engineer, building businesses, developing cybersecurity capability, and keeping semiconductor/deep-tech ambitions alive without trying to pursue five careers simultaneously.

The Strategy

Do not try to become everything at once. Engineering is the foundation. One business is the active founder experiment. Cybersecurity is a secondary technical specialization. Semiconductors are a long-term deep-tech option. Certifications support the plan rather than becoming the plan.

Build the next thing that creates evidence — not the next thing that makes the roadmap look impressive.
PRIMARY

🎓 Engineering

Your degree and technical fundamentals are the foundation everything else can build upon.

ACTIVE BUSINESS

🚀 One Business

Test one market and one problem at a time. Current hypothesis: trades/industrial automation.

SECONDARY

🔐 Cybersecurity

Develop cybersecurity capability for future optionality. Do not turn it into a second startup right now.

PARKED

🧠 Semiconductors

Keep the long-term ambition alive, but don't run a full chip-design curriculum during your initial university years.

SELECTIVE

🏅 Certifications

Target roughly 2–3 meaningful certifications across university, unless a specific opportunity justifies another.

TOOL

🤖 AI

Use AI to accelerate engineering, cybersecurity, research, coding, automation and business rather than treating AI as another separate career.

The real objective Become an engineer who can identify problems, build solutions, sell them, learn from failure, and eventually recognize opportunities large enough to support a much bigger company.

🚀 Active Business — The Founder Laboratory

This is the only business track that receives serious execution priority right now. The goal is not to predict your billion-dollar company. The goal is to accumulate founder reps.

Current business hypothesis Trades / industrial businesses with operational problems that can initially be solved manually and later productized.

Stage 1 — Customer Discovery

20

Customer conversations

This is the immediate gate. Do not build the full product before understanding the problem.

1

Target market

Stay focused enough that you can understand the economics and workflow.

1

Core problem

Find a painful problem people already spend money, time or labor solving.

  • Define the target customer
  • Build a list of 30–50 potential businesses
  • Contact the first 10 businesses
  • Complete 20 customer conversations
  • Record current workflow and pain points
  • Identify what they currently use to solve the problem
  • Determine whether money is already being spent
  • Identify the most painful recurring problem

Stage 2 — Offer

Problem confirmed

Multiple customers independently describe a similar painful problem.

Create one offer

Define exactly what you will do, for whom, and what outcome you provide.

Sell before building

Attempt to get someone to commit to paying before spending months developing software.

Deliver manually

Use spreadsheets, scripts, AI tools, forms and simple software if necessary. Learn the workflow firsthand.

Stage 3 — Productize

  • First paying customer
  • Deliver successfully
  • Identify repeated manual work
  • Automate the repeated work
  • Reach 3 paying customers
  • Reach 5 paying customers
  • Measure retention
  • Determine whether the business is repeatable
Kill / Pivot Rule If customers don't care, don't keep adding technology to a weak problem. Change the problem, customer, offer or market.

🔐 Cybersecurity — Technical Optionality

Cybersecurity is a serious technical interest, but it is NOT the active university business right now. The objective is to build capability slowly enough that it does not compete with engineering or customer acquisition.

Time budget Approximately 3–6 hours per week during a normal semester. Reduce or pause this during exams and heavy engineering periods.

Learning Sequence

1. Linux

Become comfortable operating systems, command line, permissions, processes, files and basic administration.

2. Networking

TCP/IP, DNS, HTTP/S, routing, ports, protocols and network troubleshooting.

3. Security Fundamentals

Authentication, authorization, cryptography, vulnerabilities, threat models, security controls and incident response.

4. Practical Security

Labs, CTFs, system hardening, log analysis, vulnerability analysis and small security projects.

5. Specialization Later

Only after exposure to the field should you choose offensive security, defensive security, cloud security, application security or another path.

Cybersecurity Project Examples

  • Linux hardening project
  • Network monitoring project
  • Authentication/security project
  • Log analysis project
  • Vulnerability lab
  • Security automation script
  • Small defensive security tool
Important Do not build a cybersecurity company simply because you are learning cybersecurity. The business opportunity must independently survive customer discovery.

🏅 Certification Strategy

Certifications are supporting tools. They are not the scoreboard.

Never pursue a certification simply because you can. Pursue it because it creates a specific advantage.
Certification Priority When Reason
CompTIA Security+ HIGH Year 1–2 Strong cybersecurity foundation and useful credential.
Cisco CCST Networking OPTIONAL Year 1 Useful if you need structured networking fundamentals.
AWS Solutions Architect – Associate CONDITIONAL Year 2+ Take only if cloud becomes relevant to internships/projects.
CySA+ / Security Specialty CONDITIONAL Year 3+ Only after practical cybersecurity experience.
OSCP SPECIALIZATION Year 3–4+ Only if offensive security becomes a serious direction.
CISSP LONG TERM Post-graduation Requires professional experience; not an undergraduate priority.
Target Roughly 2–3 meaningful certifications across university is enough. There is no requirement to fill every year with a certificate.

Before Starting Any Certification

  • Does it help me obtain a specific opportunity?
  • Does it teach knowledge I genuinely need?
  • Is the credential recognized by the employers I care about?
  • Have I considered learning the material without the certification?
  • Will it take time away from a higher-value project?

🚨 Anti-Distraction Rules

These rules exist specifically to prevent ambition from turning into uncontrolled scope.

RULE 01

One Active Business

You can have ideas. You cannot actively build five businesses at once.

RULE 02

Sell Before Building

Don't spend months coding a solution before confirming the problem.

RULE 03

Customer Evidence Beats Opinions

Your assumptions are hypotheses. Customer behavior is evidence.

RULE 04

Certifications Are Not Progress

A certificate only matters when it creates knowledge, credibility or access that you actually need.

RULE 05

No Parallel Careers

You are not simultaneously becoming a cybersecurity engineer, semiconductor engineer, AI researcher and founder.

RULE 06

Engineering Comes First

During university, the degree remains the foundation. The business must fit around it.

RULE 07

Ideas Go in the Parking Lot

Record new ideas instead of immediately switching directions. Revisit them at quarterly reviews.

RULE 08

Don't Optimize the Roadmap Forever

Once the plan is good enough, execution becomes more valuable than another strategy session.

The roadmap is finished when it is good enough to tell you what to do next. After that, go do it.

The Anti-Distraction List

  • Rebuilding the roadmap again
  • Adding another certification because it looks impressive
  • Starting another startup idea before testing the current one
  • Building an MVP before customer discovery
  • Learning another framework without a project requiring it
  • Starting advanced semiconductor topics prematurely
  • Treating AI as another separate career
  • Confusing research with customer validation
