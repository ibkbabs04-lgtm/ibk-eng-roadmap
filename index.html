Engineering × Founder Master Roadmap

A four-year system for becoming an engineer who can build software, hardware, AI systems, secure systems, physical products and businesses — without trying to master everything simultaneously.

🎓 Engineering 💰 Entrepreneurship 💻 Programming 🤖 AI 🔐 Cybersecurity ⚡ Electronics 🧊 3D / CAD 🧠 Semiconductors

Your actual mission

Do not try to become an expert in everything. Become exceptionally good at learning, building, selling and adapting.

The priority hierarchy:
University → Build skills → Start small businesses → Build products → Develop technical specialization → Deep-tech later.
1 Active business at a time
4 Years of university
5+ Serious engineering projects
2–3 Meaningful certifications

Weekly priority system

🎓 1. Engineering

Your degree comes first. Strong fundamentals compound for decades.

  • Attend lectures
  • Complete labs
  • Master difficult concepts
  • Build relationships with professors
  • Protect GPA without obsessing over perfection

💰 2. Business

One active business experiment. Do not run five startups simultaneously.

  • Talk to customers
  • Find painful problems
  • Sell manually
  • Deliver manually
  • Automate only after demand exists

💻 3. Technical Foundation

Programming, Linux, Git, systems, data and engineering tools.

🤖 4. AI Leverage

Learn how AI changes engineering and business — and learn to use it as leverage.

⚡ 5. Build Things

Electronics, Arduino, robotics, CAD and 3D printing turn knowledge into physical capability.

🧠 6. Deep Tech

Cybersecurity and semiconductors are long-term specialization tracks, not simultaneous businesses.

Strategy: Prevent ambition from becoming scope creep

The goal is not to finish this roadmap. The goal is to use it as a decision system.

Rule 1 — One active business

You can explore many problems, but only one gets your serious founder time at once.

Rule 2 — Build before scaling

Sell manually before writing a complicated product.

Rule 3 — Customers beat ideas

A mediocre solution to an expensive problem is usually better than a brilliant solution nobody wants.

Rule 4 — Projects beat tutorials

Every major technical concept should eventually become something you build.

Rule 5 — Certifications are supporting evidence

Certifications should support internships, employment or credibility — not become the goal.

Rule 6 — Deep tech compounds slowly

Do not rush into ASIC design or advanced cybersecurity before your engineering fundamentals are strong.

The anti-distraction test

Before adding a new goal

+
  • Does this improve my engineering ability?
  • Does this help my current business?
  • Does this create a portfolio project?
  • Does this meaningfully improve future optionality?
  • Can I realistically give it consistent time?

If the answer is mostly no, put it in the parking lot.

💰 Business Track

The purpose of university is not to build a billion-dollar company immediately. It is to get four years of entrepreneurial reps while your downside is relatively low.

Business philosophy: Find a painful problem → sell a simple solution → deliver manually → learn → improve → automate → repeat.

Phase 1 — Problem discovery

+
  • Choose one customer category
  • Identify expensive recurring problems
  • Conduct 10 customer conversations
  • Conduct 20+ conversations before major building
  • Identify existing alternatives
  • Determine what customers already pay for

Phase 2 — Manual offer

+
  • Define one clear outcome
  • Create a simple offer
  • Contact potential customers
  • Attempt to charge money
  • Deliver manually
  • Record every objection

Phase 3 — Productize

+
  • Identify repetitive work
  • Automate the repetitive parts
  • Build a basic MVP
  • Keep talking to customers
  • Measure retention
  • Improve the offer

Phase 4 — Scale or kill

+
  • Is there repeat demand?
  • Can the process be taught?
  • Are customers staying?
  • Is acquisition becoming repeatable?
  • Is the market large enough?
  • Scale, pivot or shut it down
Important: A failed business during university is not necessarily failure. A failed business where you learned how customers buy, what they value and how to sell is an extremely valuable engineering education.

🤖 AI Era — Learn How to Work With AI

This section is intentionally different from a traditional AI curriculum. The goal is not to become an AI researcher immediately. The goal is to become an engineer who is dramatically more productive because they understand how to use AI.

AI-assisted coding

  • Use AI to explain unfamiliar code
  • Use AI for debugging
  • Generate tests
  • Refactor code with AI
  • Review AI-generated code yourself

Automation

  • Understand APIs
  • Webhooks
  • Workflow automation
  • n8n-style automation
  • Connect AI to business processes

AI product building

  • Prompt engineering
  • Structured outputs
  • Retrieval concepts
  • Embeddings
  • AI API integration
  • Evaluation

AI Projects

Project 1 — Personal AI Assistant

Build an assistant that interacts with your notes, tasks and files.

Project 2 — AI Business Automation

Take a repetitive business workflow and automate part of it using APIs and AI.

Project 3 — AI Engineering Tutor

Create a tool that explains engineering problems, quizzes you and tracks weak areas.

Project 4 — AI Developer Workflow

Create your own repeatable AI-assisted development workflow.

Project 5 — AI + Hardware

Create a physical device that uses AI for a useful task.

AI principle: Don't ask "How can AI replace what I know?" Ask "What can I build now that would have been too expensive or difficult before AI?"

📜 Certification Strategy

Certifications are deliberately limited. Your projects and engineering ability matter more than collecting credentials.

Year Target Purpose Priority
Year 1 Optional foundational credential Only if useful for internships or structured learning. Low
Year 2 Security+ OR equivalent security foundation Useful if cybersecurity becomes a serious specialization. Medium
Year 3 Cloud/security certification if justified Only if your projects, internship or career direction requires it. Optional
Year 4 No automatic certification Prioritize internships, research, portfolio and business. Low
Certification rule: Never study for a certification simply because the roadmap says you should. Ask what opportunity it unlocks.
  • Does this certification appear in job postings I care about?
  • Does it help me obtain an internship?
  • Does it structure knowledge I genuinely need?
  • Can I demonstrate the same knowledge through a project?

🎓 Four-Year Execution Plan

The roadmap changes emphasis every year. You should not try to do everything at full intensity.

YEAR 1 — Foundation + First Business Experiments

+

Engineering

  • Survive the transition to university successfully
  • Learn effective study systems
  • Master introductory engineering mathematics
  • Build relationships with classmates

Programming

  • Python fundamentals
  • Git/GitHub
  • Basic web development
  • Complete 3–5 projects

Business

  • Conduct 20+ customer conversations
  • Test several problems
  • Pick one business experiment
  • Attempt first sale

Electronics / CAD

  • Learn Arduino
  • Learn basic electronics
  • Learn basic CAD
  • Print first functional part
  • Build first robot/electronics project

YEAR 2 — Build + Technical Depth

+
  • Improve Python
  • Learn JavaScript/TypeScript
  • Build full-stack project
  • Learn Linux fundamentals
  • Begin cybersecurity foundations
  • Build more advanced electronics projects
  • Design custom 3D-printed assemblies
  • Improve business based on customer feedback
  • Attempt consistent revenue

YEAR 3 — Specialization + Scale

+
  • Choose strongest technical specialization
  • Build portfolio-level engineering project
  • Develop stronger cybersecurity knowledge
  • Explore embedded systems
  • Explore FPGA/HDL
  • Build advanced robot/device
  • Pursue serious internship
  • Scale or replace the business based on evidence

YEAR 4 — Leverage + Launch

+
  • Graduate with strong engineering portfolio
  • Have 3–5 excellent projects
  • Have meaningful internship experience
  • Decide whether business deserves full-time focus
  • Decide whether cybersecurity deserves deep specialization
  • Decide whether semiconductor path deserves serious pursuit
  • Build final flagship project
  • Build professional network
