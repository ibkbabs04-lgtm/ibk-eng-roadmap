Engineering + Founder Roadmap

A four-year operating system for becoming an engineer, building businesses, developing cybersecurity capability, using AI as leverage, and keeping semiconductor ambitions alive without trying to pursue five careers simultaneously.

The Strategy

Do not try to become everything at once. Engineering is the foundation. One business is the active founder experiment. Cybersecurity is a secondary technical specialization. AI is leverage. Semiconductors are a long-term deep-tech option. Certifications support the plan rather than becoming the plan.

Build the next thing that creates evidence — not the next thing that makes the roadmap look impressive.
PRIMARY

🎓 Engineering

Your degree and technical fundamentals are the foundation everything else can build upon.

ACTIVE BUSINESS

🚀 One Business

Test one market and one problem at a time. Current hypothesis: trades / industrial automation.

SECONDARY

🔐 Cybersecurity

Develop cybersecurity capability for future optionality. Do not turn it into a second startup right now.

LEVERAGE

🤖 AI

Use AI to dramatically increase your engineering, research, automation and founder output.

PARKED

🧠 Semiconductors

Keep the long-term ambition alive without running a complete chip-design curriculum immediately.

SELECTIVE

🏅 Certifications

Roughly 2–3 meaningful certifications over university, unless a specific opportunity justifies another.

The real objective Become an engineer who can identify problems, build solutions, sell them, learn from failure, and eventually recognize opportunities large enough to support a much bigger company.
The university advantage University gives you a relatively controlled environment to experiment. You can attempt businesses, fail, restart, learn technical skills and build a network while your primary responsibility remains your education.

🚀 Active Business — Founder Laboratory

This is the only business track receiving serious execution priority right now. The goal is not to predict your billion-dollar company. The goal is to accumulate founder reps.

Current hypothesis Trades / industrial businesses with operational problems that can initially be solved manually and later productized. This is a hypothesis — not a permanent commitment.

Kill / Pivot Rule If customers do not care, do not add more technology to a weak problem. Change the problem, customer, offer or market.

🔐 Cybersecurity — Technical Optionality

Cybersecurity is a serious technical interest, but it is not your active university business right now. The objective is to build capability gradually.

Time budget Approximately 3–6 hours per week during a normal semester. Reduce or pause this during exams.

Learning Sequence

1. Linux

Operating systems, command line, permissions, processes, files and administration.

2. Networking

TCP/IP, DNS, HTTP/S, routing, ports and protocols.

3. Security Fundamentals

Authentication, authorization, cryptography, vulnerabilities, threat models and security controls.

4. Practical Security

Labs, CTFs, system hardening, logs, vulnerability analysis and small projects.

5. Specialization Later

Only after exposure should you decide between offensive security, defensive security, cloud security, application security or another specialty.

Project Ideas

  • Linux hardening project
  • Network monitoring project
  • Authentication/security project
  • Log analysis project
  • Vulnerability lab
  • Security automation script
  • Small defensive security tool
Important distinction Learning cybersecurity does not automatically mean you should build a cybersecurity company. The business opportunity must independently survive customer discovery.

🤖 AI Leverage — Learning to Win in an AI-Driven World

AI is not another career track in this roadmap. It is a force multiplier for your engineering, business, research and cybersecurity work.

Don't compete with AI. Learn how to become the person who can direct, integrate and build with it.
The objective You do NOT need to become an AI researcher. You want to become an engineer/founder who can: build with AI → automate with AI → reason with AI → integrate AI → create products using AI.

🏅 Certification Strategy

Certifications are supporting tools. They are not the scoreboard.

Never pursue a certification simply because you can. Pursue it because it creates a specific advantage.
Certification Priority Timing Reason
CompTIA Security+ HIGH Year 1–2 Strong cybersecurity foundation and useful credential.
Cisco CCST Networking OPTIONAL Year 1 Useful if you need structured networking fundamentals.
AWS Solutions Architect Associate CONDITIONAL Year 2+ Only if cloud becomes relevant.
CySA+ / Security Specialty CONDITIONAL Year 3+ Only after practical experience.
OSCP SPECIALIZATION Year 3–4+ Only if offensive security becomes a serious direction.
CISSP LONG TERM Post-graduation Professional experience makes this more appropriate later.
Target Roughly 2–3 meaningful certifications over university is enough. There is no requirement to fill every year with certificates.

Certification Gate

  • Does this help obtain a specific opportunity?
  • Does it teach knowledge I genuinely need?
  • Is the credential recognized by the employers I care about?
  • Could I learn the material without taking the exam?
  • Will it take time away from a higher-value project?

🚨 Anti-Distraction Rules

These rules exist specifically to prevent ambition from turning into uncontrolled scope.

RULE 01

One Active Business

You can have ideas. You cannot actively build five businesses simultaneously.

RULE 02

Sell Before Building

Don't spend months coding a solution before confirming the problem.

RULE 03

Customer Evidence Beats Opinions

Your assumptions are hypotheses. Customer behavior is evidence.

RULE 04

Certifications Are Not Progress

A certificate matters only when it creates knowledge, credibility or access that you actually need.

RULE 05

No Parallel Careers

You are not simultaneously becoming a cybersecurity expert, ASIC designer, AI researcher and founder.

RULE 06

Engineering Comes First

The degree remains the foundation. The business fits around it.

RULE 07

Ideas Go in the Parking Lot

Record new ideas instead of immediately switching directions. Review them quarterly.

RULE 08

AI Is a Tool

Do not chase every new AI tool. Learn transferable capabilities.

RULE 09

Don't Optimize Forever

Once the roadmap is good enough, execution becomes more valuable than another redesign.

RULE 10

Evidence Can Change the Plan

This roadmap is a system, not a contract. Real-world evidence can change your priorities.

The roadmap is finished when it is good enough to tell you what to do next. After that: Go do it.

Anti-Distraction List

  • Rebuilding the roadmap again
  • Adding certifications because they look impressive
  • Starting another startup before testing the current one
  • Building an MVP before customer discovery
  • Learning another framework without a project requiring it
  • Starting advanced semiconductor topics prematurely
  • Treating AI as another separate career
  • Chasing every new AI tool
  • Confusing research with customer validation
  • Using learning as an excuse to avoid selling
