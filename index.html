ONE ACTIVE BUSINESS

Business Lab

Do not try to build a billion-dollar company immediately. Learn how to find pain, sell a solution, deliver value and repeat.

The university advantage: You have relatively low downside, access to thousands of potential users, time to experiment and permission to fail.

🚦 Business Validation Gates

Gate 1

Talk to 10 potential customers.

Gate 2

Confirm a painful, repeated problem.

Gate 3

Deliver a manual solution.

Gate 4

Get the first dollar.

Gate 5

Reach 5 paying customers.

Gate 6

Find repeatable acquisition.

Gate 7

Scale, pivot or kill.

Founder Rules

Sell before scaling

Manually deliver the result before spending months building software.

Market > Idea

Look for existing spending, painful problems and reachable customers.

One offer

One customer, one problem, one offer before expanding.

Proof > Complexity

A simple product with five customers beats a sophisticated product with zero.

OPTIONALITY

Cybersecurity

Cybersecurity is a technical specialization and future optionality. It is not automatically the active business.

Years 1–2 — Foundation

  • Linux
  • Networking
  • Python
  • Git
  • Operating systems
  • Security fundamentals
  • Hands-on labs

Years 2–3 — Security Engineering

  • Authentication
  • Web security
  • Cloud security
  • Secure software
  • Threat modeling
  • Logging & monitoring

Years 3–4 — Choose One

  • Cloud security
  • Application security
  • Offensive security
  • Security engineering
  • Embedded / IoT security
Scope rule: Do not simultaneously become an expert in cloud security, penetration testing, malware analysis, application security and embedded security.

CREDENTIALS

Certifications

Certifications are supporting assets. They should never become the main definition of progress.

Certification decision system: Never ask "What certification should I collect next?" Ask "What specific opportunity makes this certification worth the time?"
Question Yes / No
Does an internship/job I want require it? ☐
Does it teach something I actually need? ☐
Will it materially improve credibility? ☐
Can I demonstrate the knowledge through projects instead? ☐
Is the opportunity cost worth it? ☐

Suggested Certification Strategy

Year 1

Optional: one foundational credential only if it supports an immediate goal.

Year 2

Security+ or equivalent if cybersecurity becomes a serious specialization.

Year 3

Only add a cloud/security certification if an internship or specialization justifies it.

Year 4

No certification collecting. Prioritize engineering proof, internships and business results.

NORTH STAR

Four-Year Plan

The objective changes every year. Do not try to graduate with everything mastered.

YEAR 1 — Foundation + First Business

  • Survive and learn university fundamentals.
  • Build programming foundations.
  • Learn AI-assisted workflows.
  • Talk to customers.
  • Run business experiments.
  • Try to get the first dollar.

YEAR 2 — Repeatability

  • Become technically stronger.
  • Develop Linux/networking/security foundations.
  • Determine whether one business has traction.
  • Build stronger portfolio projects.

YEAR 3 — Leverage

  • Technical specialization.
  • Internship/research.
  • Automate business operations.
  • Scale, pivot or replace the business.

YEAR 4 — Launch

  • Graduate with strong engineering credibility.
  • Have serious technical proof.
  • Have industry experience.
  • Have a business capable of continuing beyond university.
Ultimate objective: Leave university with multiple forms of leverage: technical skill + industry credibility + AI leverage + founder judgment + optionality.
